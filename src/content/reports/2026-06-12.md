## 📚 Learning Corner: P/E Ratios — Don't Compare Apples to Oranges

Look at this week's portfolio:
- **AA: P/E 18** — cheap cyclical commodity stock
- **MRVL: P/E 86** — growth semi, priced for future earnings
- **IREN: P/E 177** — essentially priced for 2028 earnings not 2026

The P/E ratio measures what you're paying per dollar of earnings **right now**. But companies at different stages of growth deserve different P/E multiples:

| Type | Typical P/E | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mature (MSFT, AA) | 15-25 | Steady but slow growth |
| Growth (MRVL, AVGO) | 50-90 | High expected earnings growth |
| Speculative (IREN) | 100+ | Betting on future profitability |

**The mistake:** Looking at IREN's P/E of 177 and thinking "oVeRpRiCeD." Yes, it is — if you only look at current earnings. But IREN is investing in AI data center infrastructure (the Microsoft $9.7B deal) that won't fully contribute to earnings until 2027-2028. The P/E is backward-looking; the AI cloud pivot is forward-looking.

**Bottom line:** P/E is useful for comparing companies in the same sector at the same stage. Cross-sector P/E comparisons (AA vs MRVL) are meaningless. Focus on P/E relative to the company's own history and sector peers.
