## News & Analysis

Tesla was the clear session standout: the EV maker delivered 486,532 vehicles in Q3, easily beating expectations of ~461,000, and the stock jumped +4.65% on Friday. Nvidia also notched a record high, pushing its market value to ~$5.7 trillion, supported by a $150 billion buyback and a weak jobs report that cooled bets on further Fed tightening.

The AI spend debate continued to animate the week. Nvidia's Huang defended AI capex, Alphabet's Pichai emphasized the Gemini ramp, and Microsoft's Nadella framed Copilot as a "new OS for work." Meanwhile Big Tech signed a voluntary White House AI safety accord. On the caution side, Morgan Stanley's Mike Wilson flagged a potential 5-10% drawdown, noting the share of S&P stocks above their 200-day moving average fell from ~75% to below 50% — a fragile-breadth concern as megacaps carry the index.

Apple got mixed coverage: an AT&T issue required some iPhone 18 Pro Max replacements after a service-loss bug, Jefferies read resale pricing as a sign of softer iPhone 18 demand, yet the stock still rose +1.02%.

## Learning Segment

Today's tape is a live case study in momentum vs. valuation. TSLA's +4.65% delivery-beat rally and NVDA's record high show how fundamental catalysts can reprice a name fast — but at P/Es of 333.9 and 29.2 respectively, price has already run far ahead of trailing earnings. Contrast this with TCS.NS (+1.19% at a 15.1 P/E) or GOOGL (17.0), where growth is still reasonably priced. Volume data was thin for the day, so the takeaway is about catalyst-driven gaps: news can override technicals in the short term, making position sizing against rich multiples the real risk discipline.
