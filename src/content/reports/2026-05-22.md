## 📚 Learning Corner: P/E Compression — When a Stock Gets "Cheaper" While Going Up

You've probably noticed NVDA's P/E ratio has dropped from ~55x to ~34x over the past year, while the stock price went up. How does a stock get more expensive and cheaper at the same time?

**It's simple math:**

P/E = Price ÷ Earnings Per Share (EPS)

If **earnings grow faster than the stock price**, the P/E ratio compresses. NVDA's earnings exploded as AI GPU demand went vertical — revenue quadrupled while the stock "only" doubled. Result: P/E halved.

| Scenario | Price | EPS | P/E |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan 2025 | $150 | $2.70 | 55x |
| Now | $219 | $6.50 | 34x |

**Why this matters:** A compressing P/E means the stock is *growing into its valuation*. It's less risky than it looks because earnings are catching up. But watch out for the reverse — if earnings disappoint, a compressed P/E can re-expand quickly in the wrong direction (stock drops faster than earnings adjust).

**Rule of thumb:** P/E compression = earnings winning. P/E expansion = multiple getting stretched. The former is healthy, the latter is speculative.
