## 📖 Learning Corner: Breadth vs. Price Divergence

When the S&P 500 hits a new record but most sectors are falling, that's called **negative breadth divergence**. It means a narrowing group of stocks is driving the index higher.

On Friday, 8 of 11 S&P sectors closed lower even as the index hit an all-time high. This is like a football team winning 1-0 on a single lucky goal while getting outplayed everywhere else — technically a win, but not sustainable.

**Why it matters:** Historically, sustained new highs with deteriorating breadth have preceded corrections. The top 25 stocks now represent 52% of the S&P 500's total weight. When those 25 stocks finally stumble, there's not much underneath to catch the fall.

**What to watch:** Track the NYSE Advance-Decline line. If it diverges from price for weeks, that's a warning. The S&P 500 Equal Weight Index (RSP) is another tool — if it's lagging the cap-weighted S&P 500, breadth is deteriorating.
