## 📚 Learning Corner — P/E Ratio and Earnings Beats

When NVDA beats earnings by $2.4B and guides $3.6B above consensus, why doesn't the stock just go up 20%?

**It's about expectations, not just numbers.**

The **P/E ratio** (Price-to-Earnings) tells you what the market is *willing* to pay for each dollar of earnings. Before earnings, NVDA traded at ~45x P/E. After a beat that big, you'd think P/E expands (stock goes up). But:

- **If the beat was already priced in** ("buy the rumor, sell the news"), the stock can stay flat or dip even on great numbers
- **If guidance is strong but margins shrink** (NVDA's Q2 op-ex guidance of $8.3B vs $7.93B est.), the market focuses on the cost side
- **Multiple compression** from macro rates (30Y yields at 2007 highs) reduces what investors will pay for any stock, even NVDA

**Key takeaway:** Earnings beats matter less than *how much* of the beat was already priced in. The market prices the *future*, not the past. NVDA's $91B Q2 guide is the real story — it tells you Q2 will be even bigger.
