- Texans learned data centers could consume **9% of state water** by 2040
- People would rather have **nuclear power plants** in their neighborhood than AI data centers

**What This Means for Investors:**

The "build AI data centers anywhere" era is ending. Regulatory friction, water scarcity, and grid constraints will slow the hyperscaler capex boom — but it won't stop it. Instead, it creates a **two-tier market**:

1. **Incumbents with secured power/water** (AMZN, MSFT, GOOGL) gain competitive moats. New entrants face years of permitting.
2. **Nuclear and renewable energy** become the only politically viable power sources. This is **massively bullish for CEG** and uranium plays.
3. **Smaller AI cloud providers** (like NBIS) that can secure stranded power assets have an edge over hyperscalers facing public opposition.
4. **Water infrastructure and efficiency tech** will become investable themes — though pure-plays are scarce.

**The Bottom Line:** Data centers aren't going away. But the *where* and *how* is changing. Companies with pre-approved nuclear sites, renewable PPAs, and community buy-in will win. Those trying to bulldoze into water-stressed regions will face delays, cost overruns, and regulatory hell.

## 📚 Learning Corner: Support and Resistance

**The Concept:** Support and resistance are price levels where a stock has historically struggled to fall below (support) or rise above (resistance). They're not magic — they reflect collective human psychology. Traders who missed buying at a low price place limit orders there, creating a "floor." Sellers who regret not selling at a peak place orders there, creating a "ceiling."

**Today's Example — MSFT:**
Microsoft trading at "January 2024 prices" is a perfect case study. If MSFT has bounced off that price level multiple times, that zone becomes **support**. The longer it holds, the stronger the signal that buyers are defending that level. A clean break below support on high volume is a warning sign. A bounce off support with increasing volume is bullish confirmation.

**How to Use It:**
- Don't buy *at* resistance — wait for a breakout or a pullback to support
- Place stop-losses just below support (not exactly at it, or you'll get "wicked out")
- Support, once broken, often becomes resistance — and vice versa

**Pro Tip:** Combine with volume. A support test on low volume = weak conviction. A support test on high volume with a bounce = strong conviction.
