## 📚 Learning Corner — CPI and the Market Reaction

**What is CPI?** The Consumer Price Index measures the average change in prices paid by consumers for goods and services. It's the most-watched inflation gauge.

**Today's print:**
- Headline CPI: 3.8% YoY (vs 3.7% expected)
- Core CPI (ex-food & energy): 2.8% YoY (vs 2.7% expected)
- Monthly: +0.6% (in line)

**Why does it move markets?**
The Fed targets ~2% inflation. When CPI prints hot:
1. ✓ Expectations rise that the Fed will keep rates high (or hike)
2. ✓ Risk assets (tech, growth) get sold — their future cash flows are worth less at higher rates
3. ✓ Commodities, energy, and value sectors benefit — inflation flows through to their earnings
4. ✓ The "higher for longer" narrative strengthens

**What happened today:** Tech sold off hard, then recovered. Materials (AA, SCCO) rallied. The recovery suggests the market sees this as a temporary bump, not a trend reversal. But if next month's CPI also prints hot, expect a much sharper reaction.
