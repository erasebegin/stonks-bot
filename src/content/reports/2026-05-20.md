## 📚 Learning Corner: Bond Yields & the "Risk-Free Rate"

**What is the 30-Year Treasury Yield?**

When people say the "30-year Treasury yield" is at 5.18%, they're talking about what the US government pays to borrow money for 30 years. Treasuries are considered "risk-free" because the US has never defaulted.

**Why does a rising yield matter for stocks?**

Because the risk-free rate is the *alternative* to owning stocks. If you can earn 5.18% annually for 30 years with zero risk, stocks need to offer a higher expected return to be attractive. This is called the **Equity Risk Premium** — the extra return you demand for taking stock market risk.

When bond yields rise:
- **Growth stocks get crushed** — their future earnings are discounted more heavily (discounted cash flow math)
- **Valuation multiples compress** — the P/E ratio investors are willing to pay shrinks
- **Borrowing gets more expensive** — companies that rely on debt for growth (tech, real estate) suffer
- **Money leaves emerging markets** — why take currency risk in India/Turkey when you can get 5.18% in dollars?

**Why is 5.18% so significant?**

The last time yields were this high was ~2007, right before the financial crisis. The 2020s saw yields at 1-2%. The jump from 2% to 5%+ means the entire valuation framework for stocks has shifted. It's the macro story that underpins everything else right now.
