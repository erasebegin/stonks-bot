## Learning Corner: Understanding P/E Ratio

**P/E (Price-to-Earnings) Ratio** = Stock Price ÷ Earnings Per Share (EPS)

**What it tells you:** How many years of current earnings it would take to pay back your investment (if earnings stay the same).

**Quick reference:**
- **P/E < 15:** Value/undervalued (MSFT ~25, META ~22 are reasonable)
- **P/E 15-25:** Fair value for a growing company
- **P/E 25-40:** Growth premium (NVDA ~33, GOOGL ~30)
- **P/E > 40:** High growth expectations (AMD ~167, MRVL ~68, PLTR ~154)
- **P/E = N/A:** Company has negative earnings (SNOW, CRWD — they're valued on revenue/growth)

**Important:** P/E is backward-looking (trailing) or forward-looking (based on analyst estimates). A high P/E means the market expects high future growth — but also leaves less room for disappointment.

**Today's example:** AMD trades at P/E 167 while MSFT trades at ~25. The market is pricing AMD for explosive growth (next-gen AI chips) while MSFT is seen as a steady compounder. If AMD delivers, 167 becomes 50 next year. If they stumble, the multiple compression hurts twice.
