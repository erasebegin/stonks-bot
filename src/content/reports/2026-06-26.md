## 🔬 Tech Deep Dive: The Micron Ripple Effect

Micron's blowout earnings (+16%) was the headline, but the real story is what it means for everyone else.

**The mechanism:** AI demand for HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) and DDR5 is soaking up all available memory/storage chip supply. Micron, Samsung, and SK Hynix have pricing power for the first time in years. But that means Apple's memory costs just spiked — hence the mid-cycle price hike on iPads, Macs, and the Vision Pro.

**Who wins:** Memory makers (Micron, Samsung) and semi equipment (ASML, Applied Materials).

**Who loses:** Hardware OEMs with thin margins (AAPL, Dell, HP) and any consumer-facing tech that can't fully pass costs.

**Bigger picture:** This is the first real "inflation" signal from the AI boom reaching the consumer price level. If iPhones get more expensive by year-end (as reports suggest), it could dent consumer demand and create a second-order drag on the entire consumer tech sector.

**The bullish counter:** For Microsoft and Google, this is a different story — they consume chips for cloud infrastructure, not for resale. Their AI revenue (Azure, Google Cloud, Copilot) has margin to absorb costs. That's why MSFT at 21x P/E post-dip is worth a close look.

## 📚 Learning Corner — RSI: The Hidden Trap

This week's tech selloff is a perfect case study for why **RSI alone can mislead you**.

Apple hit an RSI of roughly 30 (oversold territory) after the -6.12% drop. Conventional wisdom says "buy the oversold bounce." But context matters: the drop was catalyst-driven (price hike announcement), not just market noise. A stock can stay oversold while the fundamental thesis shifts.

**Key insight:** Always ask *why* RSI is low before acting. Is it:
- A panic overreaction? → Buy the dip
- A structural change in the business? → Wait for stabilisation
- End-of-quarter rebalancing? → Likely temporary

When VIX is elevated and tech is getting hit across the board, wait for RSI to base and curl up before entering. A falling knife catches more than RSI signals.
