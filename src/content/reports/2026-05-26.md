## 📚 Learning Corner: Trailing P/E Ratio

**What is it?** The trailing price-to-earnings ratio divides a stock's current price by its earnings over the past 12 months. For the S&P 500, it's the aggregate P/E of all 500 companies.

**Current snapshot:** S&P 500 at **31.8x trailing earnings** vs. long-term average of **~17.6x** and 10-year average of **~23x**.

**What does 31.8x mean?**
- You're paying $31.80 for every $1 of earnings
- At the historical average, the "fair" S&P 500 level would be ~4,350 (about 42% lower)
- At the 10-year average, fair value would be ~5,650 (about 24% lower)

**But context matters:**
1. **Low interest rates inflate P/Es** — when bonds yield little, stocks command higher multiples
2. **Index composition has changed** — tech/growth companies with higher margins now dominate the index
3. **Earnings are cyclical** — if AI-driven productivity boosts earnings, the multiple compresses naturally

**The takeaway:** High P/E doesn't mean an imminent crash. It means lower expected returns going forward, and less margin for error. When you're buying at 31.8x, you're paying for future growth. If that growth disappoints, the re-pricing can be brutal.
