## 📚 Learning Corner — Understanding Dilution and Its Impact

**What is dilution?** When a company issues new shares, each existing share represents a smaller ownership percentage. That's dilution.

**The math:**
- Company has 100M shares outstanding at $100/share = $10B market cap
- Company issues 20M new shares at $100/share, raising $2B
- Now 120M shares. If the $2B creates $2B+ in value, existing shareholders break even or gain.
- If the market decides the $2B will earn less than it costs, share price drops.

**Why GOOGL fell 1% on $80B raise:**
The market is pricing immediate dilution (~3.5% share increase at current prices) while waiting for proof the investment generates returns. But Berkshire's participation signals confidence that the ROI is real. Short-term pain, long-term gain — the classic pattern.

**Key metric to watch:** Incremental ROIC (Return on Invested Capital) on AI spend. If Google earns >15% on this $80B, it was a bargain.
